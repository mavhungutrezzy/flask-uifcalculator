How to Check UIF Status Online

You can check your UIF claim status online through the UIF's official portal. Have your South African ID number and the details you used when you registered ready before you start.

Don't confuse the two portals: The old uFiling site is now mainly used by employers to submit monthly UIF declarations. If you're an individual checking your own claim, you need UIF Online that's what this guide covers.
1

Open UIF Online

Go to the official UIF Online portal for individual claims. Need help finding your way in? Read our UIF Status Check Guide.

2

Sign in or register

Already registered? Sign in with your details. First time? You'll need to create an account with your personal information. If you submitted your claim at the Department of Labour (DOL), you will need to create your UIF Online account, and you will need your ID number and to answer some security questions.

3

Find your claim

Once you're signed in, go to View Applications, then Application for Benefit History. You will then see your claim status. For the meaning of UIF claim statuses, check the section below.

4

Check the status

Your claim will show where it is in the process. Exact wording can differ a bit depending on your claim type.

5

Check payment info

If your claim is approved, go to Request for Payment. If you have requested payment before, you may check View Payment Request. If this is the first time you are requesting payment for a continuation of benefit, go to Request for Payment. You can only access this option when your claim is approved.

UIF Status Meanings Explained

One of the most confusing parts of checking UIF is understanding what the status means. A status does not always mean that you will receive money immediately. Your claim may still need to go through several steps.

This generally means that your application has been received by the UIF system. It does not necessarily mean that your claim has been approved. Your application may still need to be checked.

Your application is being reviewed. The UIF may need to check:
  • Your identity
  • Employment information & contributions
  • Reason for claiming
  • Employer information
  • Supporting documents
  • Banking details
Processing times can vary, especially when additional information is required.

An approved claim means the UIF has accepted the claim for the relevant benefit. You do not need to request the first payment manually the first payment is usually processed automatically once your claim is approved. After that, you may request payment again every 28 days only if you still have remaining UIF credit available. Approval does not always mean the money is already in your bank account because the payment still needs to be processed.

Payment processed normally means that a payment has been processed for your claim. The time before the money appears in your bank account depends on the payment process and your bank.

Payment reserved means the money has already been processed and reserved for payment, and it may take on average 7 to 15 days to reflect in your bank account. This is a normal stage in the payment cycle.

Payment made means a payment has been recorded as made. If the payment is not showing in your bank account after a reasonable period, check your banking information and UIF claim details.

A declined claim means the UIF has not approved the claim. If your claim has been declined, read our guide on why a UIF claim gets rejected or cancelled to understand the reasons and see how to correct missing information or request a review.

This means something may still be required before your claim can continue. For example:
  • Submit a document
  • Confirm information
  • Update your banking details
  • Complete a required declaration
  • Provide additional information
Do not ignore an action required message because it can delay your claim.

Why Is My UIF Still Not Approved?

A UIF claim can remain pending or unresolved while the Department of Employment and Labour checks your application. There can be several reasons for a delay:

1

Application still being checked

UIF needs to verify the information submitted with your claim.

2

Employer information not confirmed

Some UIF processes depend on information submitted by your employer. Your employer is normally required to submit your contribution information by the 15th of each month. Also make sure your previous employer has finalised the declaration and given you your UI-19. If there are errors in your salary, start date, or end date of employment, the UI-19 can be rejected and your claim may take longer.

3

Documents are missing

There are 3 key documents you need to check: your ID, your UI-19, and the UI-2.8.1 form. The UI-2.8.1 is especially important for payment, so make sure there are no errors when filling it in. Also verify that the bank did not make errors or typos, and that the signatures you use are the same as the ones on your other forms.

4

Banking information needs attention

Incorrect or outdated banking information can cause payment problems. Make sure your banking details are correct and belong to you. You can get the banking form from the bank with a stamp to confirm the account details.

5

Contributions need checking

UIF benefits are linked to UIF contributions. If contribution records are incomplete or incorrect, your claim may need further verification.

6

Additional information needed

Sometimes the department needs more information before making a decision.

Why Has My UIF Payment Not Arrived?

If your UIF claim has been approved but you have not received your money, first check your UIF status again. Also check:

  • Whether the payment has actually been processed
  • Whether your banking details are correct
  • Whether your bank account is active
  • Whether UIF requested additional information
  • Whether there is another outstanding step on your claim

If the UIF system shows that payment has been made but you cannot find the money, you should contact the Department of Employment and Labour or UIF for assistance.

Do not submit multiple applications for the same benefit simply because your payment is delayed.

How Long Does UIF Take to Pay?

There is no single guaranteed number of days that applies to every UIF claim. In many cases, payments are usually made 7 to 15 days after the claim is approved. After that, you can request payment again every 28 days only if you still have UIF credit days remaining. Other than that, timing can vary depending on the bank and the payment process, with some payments arriving faster and others taking longer.

  • Type of UIF benefit
  • Whether the application is complete
  • Employer information
  • Contribution records
  • Supporting documents
  • Banking information
  • Whether additional verification is required

A straightforward application can move faster than one that needs corrections or additional information. If your claim has been waiting for a long time, checking the status and identifying any outstanding action is more useful than simply submitting another application.
