How Is Maternity UIF Calculated in South Africa?

Maternity UIF is calculated using a sliding income replacement rate. Most employees receive between 38% and 60% of their normal salary (capped at R17,712 per month), depending on earnings and UIF contribution records.

Your estimated maternity UIF payout depends on:

  • your normal gross salary,
  • any salary paid by your employer during leave,
  • UIF contributions (1 credit day earned for every 4 days worked),
  • and available credit days (up to 121 credit days for maternity).

1. Completely Unpaid Leave

If your employer pays 0% salary during leave, UIF pays your full Daily Benefit Amount (38%–60% of normal salary).

2. Partial Employer Pay

If your employer pays 50% or 75%, UIF tops up the difference so long as total pay does not exceed 100% of normal salary.

3. 100% Employer Pay

If your employer pays 100% full salary throughout leave, no UIF top-up benefit can be claimed.

How Long Does Maternity UIF Pay?

Maternity UIF benefits are usually paid for up to 121 days (about 4 months), depending on your available UIF credits and claim approval.

Payment periods may differ if:

  • your employer continues partial salary payments,
  • your claim is submitted late,
  • or supporting documents are missing.

How Long Does Maternity UIF Take to Pay Out?

Processing times differ between claims. Some applicants receive payments within 4 to 8 weeks, while others may wait longer depending on:

  • UIF office workload and processing backlogs,
  • document verification by the Department of Labour,
  • banking details verification (Form UI-2.8),
  • and uFiling online portal processing status.

Applying early and submitting complete documents can help reduce delays.

Can I Claim Maternity UIF After 6 Months?

Maternity UIF claims should ideally be submitted before childbirth or within 6 months after childbirth or adoption. Late applications submitted after 6 months may require additional supporting motivation letters and could delay or invalidate approval.

To avoid complications, many applicants submit claims up to 4 weeks before their expected due date.

Documents Needed for Maternity UIF Claims

Common maternity UIF documents required for submission include:

  • Form UI-19 — Employer declaration confirming employment history
  • Form UI-2.7 — Employer declaration of remuneration received during leave
  • Form UI-2.8 — Banking details confirmation form stamped by your bank
  • Form UI-2.3 — Application for maternity benefits
  • Medical Certificate / Doctor's letter — Signed by medical practitioner or midwife confirming due date or birth date
  • South African ID document or passport (13-digit bar-coded ID or Smart Card)
  • Child's Birth Certificate (if applying post-birth)

Missing or incorrect documents are the #1 cause of UIF payment delays.

Maternity UIF Forms Explained

The UI-19 form confirms your employment details and UIF contributions. The UI-2.7 form specifies how much salary (if any) your employer pays you while on leave. The UI-2.8 form verifies your bank account to prevent payment fraud.

These forms are critical because UIF uses them to calculate maternity benefits and verify your claim authorization.
