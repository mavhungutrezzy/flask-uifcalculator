1. Calculating Your UIF Payout

Before you claim, it helps to know what you are entitled to. UIF payouts depend on your salary history, how long you have contributed, and the income replacement rate set by law.

Quick tip: use our UIF Unemployment Calculator to estimate your monthly payout, credit days, and total benefit in under a minute.

The UIF formula uses a salary ceiling and your average monthly earnings over the last four(4) years. Credit days build up as you work — roughly one day of benefits for every four days worked. Want the full breakdown? Read our guide on how to calculate UIF payout or see what your numbers mean after you run them through the calculator in what to do next.

2. How to Claim UIF Online

The uFiling portal is where most UIF claims start. You will need to register, select your benefit type, upload documents, and provide banking details. It sounds simple, but small mistakes can delay your claim by weeks.

Our step-by-step guide covers how to claim UIF online — from registration through to submitting your first payment request. If you are stuck before you even reach the claim form, start with the uFiling login guide so you can get into the portal, reset access, and avoid common sign-in loops.

We also explain what to do if the system rejects your application, asks for extra documents, or sends you back to your employer for missing declarations.

3. Documents You Need

Paperwork is where most claims get stuck. The Department of Employment and Labour needs specific forms and proof before they process anything. The essentials include:

  • Your South African ID
  • The UI-19 form from your employer
  • Banking details on a UI-2.8 form
  • Proof that you are actively seeking work

See the full checklist in our UIF documents needed guide. If you are claiming maternity UIF, the requirements are slightly different — we cover those in our maternity UIF documents guide.

4. Checking Your Claim Status

Once you have submitted your claim, you can check its progress online. Status messages like “pending,” “approved,” or “payment blocked” each mean something different — and each needs a different response.

Our guide on UIF status check online explains what each status means and what action to take. If you prefer a specific lookup method, you can also read how to do a UIF status check by ID number or check your UIF status using WhatsApp.

Status checks are only one part of the picture. If you need to understand available benefit days or whether a payment has been released, our UIF balance guide explains where to look. If online options do not help, use the UIF call centre contact list to find the right support channel before visiting a labour centre.

5. Payments, Delays & Credit Days

UIF payments do not always arrive on time. Delays can happen because of missing employer declarations, incorrect banking details, or backlogs at the Department of Labour.

UIF payment dates and delays covers the most common reasons payments are held up and how long you should realistically expect to wait.

Credit days are another source of confusion. These determine how long you can receive UIF — typically up to 365 days depending on your contribution history. Learn how they work in our UIF credit days explained guide. If your employer has not been declaring your contributions, that is also a problem — read about missing UIF contributions to understand your options.

6. Common Problems & Rejections

Rejected claims are frustrating, but they are rarely permanent. Most rejections happen because of missing documents, incorrect banking details, or eligibility issues.

Our guide on why UIF claims get rejected walks through the most common rejection reasons and how to fix them. If your claim was cancelled rather than rejected, the process is different — we explain both scenarios.

7. Special Cases: Maternity, Contracts & Resignation

Maternity UIF

Maternity UIF is a separate benefit with its own forms and requirements. If you are still planning your leave, start with our guide to maternity leave in South Africa so you understand timing, employer notice, and how UIF fits around paid or unpaid leave.

When you are ready to claim, you will need medical certificates, employer confirmation, and the correct banking forms. Our maternity UIF documents guide has the full list.

Fixed-term contracts

If your fixed-term contract ends, you may still qualify for UIF — but only if your employer declared your contributions correctly. Check what to look for on your UI-19 in our guide on UIF for fixed-term contracts.

Resignation

Resignation usually disqualifies you from unemployment UIF, but there are exceptions. If you resigned under specific circumstances — such as constructive dismissal — you may still have a case. Read can I claim UIF if I resigned to see where you stand.
