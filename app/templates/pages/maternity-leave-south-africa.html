Maternity Leave South Africa 2026

How long maternity leave lasts, how much UIF pays, when to apply, what documents you need, and how to check your claim status.

UIF Calculators Team · Updated July 2026 · 9 min read

Every employee in South Africa is entitled to four consecutive months of maternity leave under the Basic Conditions of Employment Act. That leave is unpaid by law, but most mothers can claim a UIF maternity benefit paid at a flat 66% of their capped daily income, for up to 121 days.

This guide covers the legal leave entitlement, exactly how the UIF payout is worked out, who qualifies, what documents you need, and how to apply and track your claim.

How Long Is Maternity Leave in South Africa?

Under the Basic Conditions of Employment Act, every employee is legally entitled to four consecutive months of maternity leave. This applies no matter how long you have worked for your employer, and it applies whether you are full time, part time, or on a fixed term contract, as long as you are formally employed.

Maternity leave may start any time from four weeks before your expected due date, or on the day your baby is born if the birth happens earlier than planned. After the birth, you must take at least six weeks of compulsory leave before returning to work. Your employer cannot ask you to come back sooner unless a doctor confirms in writing that you are fit to do so.

Maternity leave timeline at a glance

Earliest start 4 weeks before your due date
Total entitlement 4 consecutive months, about 17 to 18 weeks
Compulsory post birth leave At least 6 weeks
Maximum UIF maternity days 121 days
UIF claim deadline Before the birth, or within 12 months after

If you lose a pregnancy in the third trimester, or your baby is stillborn, you can still claim UIF maternity benefits for six weeks. This recognises that recovery time is needed regardless of the outcome of the pregnancy.

Does Your Employer Have to Pay You During Maternity Leave?

No. South African law does not require employers to pay a salary during maternity leave. The BCEA guarantees the right to take the leave, not the right to be paid for it.

Your income during maternity leave is meant to come from the UIF maternity benefit, a payment from the Unemployment Insurance Fund, not your employer. Some employers voluntarily top up your pay, sometimes to 100% of your normal salary, but this is a company policy choice, not a legal requirement. Always check your employment contract or staff handbook for any maternity pay policy your employer offers.

If your employer does pay part of your salary while you are on leave, UIF only covers the shortfall. The combined total you receive from your employer and UIF cannot exceed 100% of what you would normally have earned.

How Much Will UIF Pay During Maternity Leave?

UIF maternity benefits are paid at a flat rate of 66% of your capped daily income. This is different from UIF's unemployment and illness benefits, which use a sliding scale of roughly 38% to 60% depending on your salary. Maternity claims use the same 66% rate for every earner, up to the salary ceiling.

Your monthly salary is capped at R17 712 for the calculation. If you earn more than this, UIF still calculates your benefit as if you earn exactly R17 712. Earners below the ceiling use their actual salary.

How the calculation works

  • Cap your monthly salary at R17 712 if you earn more than that
  • Convert it to a daily income by multiplying by 12 and dividing by 365
  • Multiply your daily income by 66% to get your daily benefit
  • Multiply your daily benefit by your paid leave days, up to 121

A common misunderstanding is expecting UIF to replace your full salary for four months. It does not. Even at the flat 66% rate, higher earners are capped at the R17 712 ceiling, so someone earning R30 000 a month receives the same benefit as someone earning exactly R17 712.

Who Qualifies for UIF Maternity Benefits?

To qualify for maternity UIF, you must:

  • Be employed and going on official maternity leave, not resigning
  • Have contributed to UIF for at least 13 weeks before your claim
  • Be registered as a UIF contributor, with 1% deducted from your salary monthly
  • Be receiving less than your normal remuneration while on leave
  • Be a South African citizen or permanent resident, or hold a valid work permit

If you resign before going on maternity leave, you forfeit your UIF maternity benefit. You need to remain formally employed and take official maternity leave to qualify. If you are unsure how resigning affects a claim more generally, see can I claim UIF if I resigned.

Who does not qualify

  • Employees who resign voluntarily before their leave starts
  • Self employed or freelance workers who are not registered for UIF
  • Workers employed for fewer than 24 hours a month for that employer
  • Anyone still receiving their full normal salary during leave, since there is no income shortfall to replace

Domestic workers are generally covered by UIF since amendments extended the fund to domestic employment, so most domestic workers who are registered and contributing can claim maternity benefits in the same way as other employees.

Documents Required for Maternity UIF

Gather these before you submit your claim to avoid delays:

  • South African ID, original plus a certified copy
  • UI-2.7 maternity claim form, completed and signed by your doctor, midwife or clinic
  • UI-19 employer declaration, completed by your employer
  • UI-2.8 banking details form, for the account payments will go into
  • A recent bank statement in your own name
  • Proof of pregnancy, such as a sick note or hospital letter, or a birth certificate after delivery

After the birth, you will also need your baby's birth certificate to continue or finalise your claim. For a broader look at what UIF typically asks for across different claim types, see UIF documents needed, and for a closer look at the maternity specific paperwork, see maternity UIF documents needed.

Banking details are one of the most common reasons a maternity payment gets held up after approval. If you are not sure your details on file are correct, see the UIF banking details and UI-2.8 guide before you apply.

How to Apply for Maternity UIF

Option A: apply online via uFiling

  • Register or log in at the official uFiling portal
  • Go to the maternity benefit section under claims
  • Complete the online application and upload your documents
  • Submit and note your claim reference number
  • Each month, submit the continuation form to keep payments coming

Option B: apply in person at a labour centre

  • Find your nearest Department of Employment and Labour office
  • Bring all original documents listed above
  • Complete the application forms at the labour centre
  • Collect your reference number before you leave

Apply early. Submitting your claim close to your due date, rather than waiting, gives the Department more time to process it before your income actually stops. Claims can take several weeks to process, and applying late simply means a longer wait before your first payment lands.

How to Check Your Maternity UIF Status

After applying, you can track your claim in a few ways:

  • Log in to uFiling and check benefit payments under your claims
  • Dial the UIF USSD code from any mobile phone and follow the prompts, no internet needed
  • Check your status using your ID number if you would rather not log in
  • Message the UIF WhatsApp line for a quick status update

For a full walkthrough of what each status message means, see the UIF status check guide, the status check by ID number, or the WhatsApp status check. If your payment seems to be taking longer than expected, the UIF payment dates and delays guide covers the most common causes and what you can do about them.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long is maternity leave in South Africa?

Four consecutive months, about 17 to 18 weeks, under the Basic Conditions of Employment Act. This applies to every employee regardless of how long they have worked for their employer.

How much maternity UIF will I receive?

You will generally receive 66% of your daily income, capped at a monthly salary of R17 712, for up to 121 days. Use the maternity UIF calculator for a figure based on your own salary.

Does my employer pay me during maternity leave?

Not by law. Maternity leave is unpaid under the BCEA, and your income is expected to come from the UIF maternity benefit unless your employer chooses to offer paid maternity leave as a company policy.

Can I claim maternity UIF if I am a contract worker?

Yes, as long as you are formally employed, registered as a UIF contributor, and have contributed for at least 13 weeks before your claim. Fixed term contract status on its own does not disqualify you.

When should I apply for maternity UIF?

As early as possible, ideally around your due date and no later than 12 months after the birth. Applying early gives the Department more time to process your claim before your income from work stops.

What happens if my employer will not complete the UI-19 form?

Your claim generally cannot be processed without the employer declaration. If your employer is unresponsive, raising it with the Department directly, or through your nearest labour centre, is usually the next step, since UIF relies on that declaration to confirm your employment and salary history.

Can fathers claim maternity UIF?

No. Maternity UIF benefits apply only to the parent who gives birth. The non birthing parent may qualify for a separate parental benefit under a different claim type, with its own documents and rules.
