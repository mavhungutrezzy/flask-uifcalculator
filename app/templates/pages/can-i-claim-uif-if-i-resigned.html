Can I Claim UIF If I Resigned?

No, generally not. UIF unemployment benefits are meant for people who lose work through no fault of their own, and resigning is treated as a choice. There's one real exception, and a few other benefit types worth knowing about.

UIF Calculators Team · Updated July 2026 · 7 min read

Short answer: if you resign voluntarily, you're treated as voluntarily unemployed and don't meet the requirements for unemployment benefits. The main exception is constructive dismissal, where the CCMA or a similar formal process confirms you were effectively forced to resign because of intolerable working conditions created by your employer.

UIF exists to support people who lose their income unexpectedly, not people who choose to leave. That's the reasoning behind the resignation rule, and it's applied fairly strictly. But "I resigned" covers a lot of different real situations, some of which do still open the door to a claim, so it's worth working through where you actually stand before assuming there's nothing you can do.

When UIF Unemployment Benefits Usually Apply

  • You were retrenched or laid off, due to your employer's operational requirements.
  • You were dismissed, subject to UIF's qualifying rules and provided you weren't disqualified for serious misconduct.
  • Your fixed-term contract expired, and wasn't renewed.
  • Your employer terminated your services for reasons other than your own resignation.

Notice the common thread: in every one of these, the decision to end your employment sat with your employer, not with you. That's the line UIF draws.

What About Constructive Dismissal?

Constructive dismissal is a specific labour law situation where you resign because your employer made continued employment genuinely intolerable, for example through sustained harassment, unpaid salary, or a unilateral and unreasonable change to your role. On paper, it looks like a resignation. In substance, labour law treats it as a dismissal.

UIF guidance treats this as a possible exception, but only once constructive dismissal has actually been established, usually through the CCMA. You can't simply tell UIF that your resignation was "basically a dismissal" and expect it to be accepted, the CCMA process is what turns that claim into something UIF can act on.

If you think this applies to you, get advice from the CCMA, the Department of Employment and Labour, or a qualified labour practitioner before you resign if at all possible, since how and why you resign can matter for the case itself.

Other Situations That Aren't Quite a Simple Resignation

A few common scenarios sit in a grey area worth knowing about, even though none of them guarantee a claim.

  • Mutual separation agreements. If you and your employer agreed to part ways and it's documented as such, UIF will usually look at how the agreement is framed, not just the word "resignation" on a form.
  • Resigning after being told to leave. If your employer pressured you to resign rather than formally dismissing you, this can shade into constructive dismissal territory, but again, it needs to go through the CCMA to count.
  • Retirement. Retiring is treated differently from resigning for other reasons, and doesn't automatically qualify for unemployment benefits either, though other retirement related provisions may apply depending on your fund.

If your situation doesn't cleanly fit "I chose to leave" or "I was let go," it's worth getting a straight answer from the CCMA or a labour practitioner before you assume either way.

Can You Claim Other UIF Benefits After Resigning?

Resignation mainly affects unemployment benefits specifically. If your situation isn't about being unemployed, a different UIF benefit type may still be relevant, and resigning from your job doesn't automatically rule these out.

  • Illness benefits: if you can't work because of illness and meet UIF's requirements, including having contributed enough credit days.
  • Maternity benefits: if you qualify for maternity UIF and receive less than your normal salary while on maternity leave.
  • Adoption or parental benefits: if the applicable UIF rules and documents apply to your situation.

What to Check Before Applying

  • The termination reason recorded on your UI-19 form, since this is what UIF actually assesses your claim against.
  • Whether your employer has submitted your UIF declarations, see our UIF documents needed checklist for the full list.
  • Whether you've registered as a work seeker with the Department of Employment and Labour, a requirement for unemployment claims.
  • Your estimated credit days and possible payout duration, if your situation does qualify.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does UIF ever pay out after resignation without going through the CCMA?

Not for standard unemployment benefits. Constructive dismissal is the recognised exception, and it needs to be established through the CCMA or a similar formal process before UIF will treat it as anything other than a voluntary resignation.

Can I claim UIF if I resigned because of my health?

Not under unemployment benefits, but illness benefits are a separate category with their own requirements. If you're unable to work due to illness, that's worth exploring on its own terms rather than through an unemployment claim.

I resigned from one job and then lost a second job through retrenchment. Can I claim?

Yes, in that case UIF looks at how your most recent employment ended, not your full work history. A retrenchment from your latest job generally qualifies, regardless of how a previous job ended.

What if my UI-19 form says "resigned" but I believe I was dismissed?

This is worth raising directly, since the termination reason on your UI-19 is what UIF assesses your claim against. Speak to your employer first to correct it if it's simply wrong, and if there's a genuine dispute about why your employment ended, that's a matter for the CCMA.

Can I still apply for UIF even if I'm not sure I qualify?

You can submit a claim and let UIF assess it, rather than assuming in advance. If you do apply and it's rejected, our guide on why UIF claims get rejected or cancelled explains what to check next.
