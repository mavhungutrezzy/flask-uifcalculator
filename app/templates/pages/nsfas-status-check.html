NSFAS Status Check: The Full Guide

How to check your NSFAS status on myNSFAS, WhatsApp or USSD, what Provisionally Funded and Unsuccessful actually mean, and what to do if your application gets turned down.

UIF Calculators Team · Updated July 2026 · 11 min read

Quick answer: log into my.nsfas.org.za with your ID number and password to see your status. No data? Send your ID number to NSFAS on WhatsApp at +27 63 093 5671, or dial *120*67327# from any cellphone. All three show the same underlying status, just through different channels.

NSFAS pays for tuition, accommodation, transport and study materials for students who could not otherwise afford to study. For most students it does not need to be paid back, which is exactly why the application and status process gets so much attention every year. This guide covers every way to check your status, what each result actually means, and what to do next if something looks wrong or your application was turned down.

What Is NSFAS and Who Qualifies

NSFAS stands for the National Student Financial Aid Scheme. It is a South African government programme that funds students at the country's 26 public universities and 50 TVET colleges. For most funded students, it works as a bursary, meaning it does not need to be repaid, as long as you keep meeting the academic and registration requirements each year.

To qualify for the standard bursary, your combined household income needs to be R350,000 a year or less. If you're living with a disability, that threshold moves up to R600,000 a year. NSFAS also introduced a loan option for what's often called the missing middle, students whose household income sits above R350,000 but at or below R600,000. Unlike the bursary, this loan is repayable once you're working, but it still opens the door to funding for households that earn too much for the bursary but still can't easily afford tuition on their own.

One more thing worth knowing early is the N+2 rule. NSFAS generally funds you for the normal duration of your qualification, plus two extra years if you need them. A three year degree, for example, would typically be funded for up to five years in total. Exactly how this is applied can depend on your specific programme and institution, so confirm the details with your financial aid office if you're not sure where you stand.

How to Check Your NSFAS Status

There are three main ways to check, and all of them need your ID number.

myNSFAS portal

  1. Go to my.nsfas.org.za.
  2. Log in with your ID number and password. If you've forgotten your password, use the reset option and follow the prompts sent to your registered email or phone.
  3. Your dashboard shows your current status, along with any action items NSFAS needs from you, like outstanding documents.
  4. If you've been approved, you'll also see a funding agreement to sign here before any money can be paid out.

This is the most complete option, since it shows action items and document requests that WhatsApp and USSD don't always display in full.

WhatsApp

Save +27 63 093 5671 to your phone, open WhatsApp, and send your ID number with a short message asking for your status. This is often the fastest option if you're just after a quick update and don't want to log in anywhere.

USSD, for phones without data

Dial *120*67327# on any cellphone, enter your ID number when asked, and follow the menu. This works without airtime for data or a smartphone, though the service can occasionally be offline during very busy periods, in which case WhatsApp or the portal are good backups.

What Each NSFAS Status Means

Received / Submitted NSFAS has your application and it's waiting in the queue for eligibility checks. No action needed from you yet.
Provisionally Funded / Conditionally Approved You've cleared the eligibility checks, but NSFAS is waiting for your institution to confirm your registration before this becomes final.
Funding Eligible / Approved Your funding is confirmed. NSFAS will pay your institution directly for tuition, and your allowances will follow the usual payment schedule.
Unsuccessful Your application didn't meet the requirements, most often because of the income threshold, institution type, or a previous qualification. See the next section for the full list of reasons.

If your status has been sitting on Provisionally Funded for a while, it usually means your institution hasn't yet confirmed your registration with NSFAS. This typically clears up seven to fourteen days after registration is finalised on their end, so check in with your institution's financial aid office if it's been longer than that.

Why Applications Get Declined

  • Household income above the threshold. Your combined household income is above R350,000 a year, or above R600,000 if you applied under the disability or missing middle categories.
  • Studying at a private institution. NSFAS only funds the 26 public universities and 50 TVET colleges, not private colleges.
  • Already completed a qualification. If NSFAS has already funded you through to a completed qualification at that level, a new application at the same level is usually declined.
  • Missing or incorrect documents. Incomplete supporting documents, or details that don't match Home Affairs records, can cause a decline that's often fixable on appeal.
  • Academic exclusion. If you've been academically excluded by your institution, this can affect your funding status independently of your financial eligibility.

How to Appeal an Unsuccessful Application

An Unsuccessful status is not always final. If you believe the decision was wrong, or your circumstances have changed, you can usually appeal directly through myNSFAS.

  1. Log into my.nsfas.org.za and look for the appeal option linked to your application.
  2. Write a short, clear motivation explaining why you believe you should qualify, and attach supporting documents, such as proof of income, a death certificate if a parent's income changed due to loss, or corrected ID documents.
  3. If your appeal relates to academic exclusion, include an academic improvement plan where possible, some institutions ask for this before they'll support reinstatement.
  4. Track your appeal's progress through the same portal. If you haven't heard anything after 30 days, follow up directly by email or phone rather than waiting indefinitely.

Appeals involving documentation errors are usually quicker to resolve than appeals about academic exclusion, since the second type often needs manual review by your institution as well as NSFAS.

Allowances and Payments

Once you're approved, NSFAS pays your tuition fees directly to your institution, so you'll never see that portion in your own account. Everything else works differently.

  • Accommodation allowance. Paid either directly to your accredited residence or into your NSFAS wallet, depending on your living arrangement.
  • Living allowance. Covers day to day costs like food, and is paid into your NSFAS wallet on a set schedule.
  • Transport allowance. For students who qualify, based on distance from campus and living arrangement.
  • Book and learning material allowance. Usually paid once per academic year, or per semester depending on your institution.

Make sure your banking details or NSFAS wallet linkage are correct and up to date on myNSFAS, since incorrect details are one of the most common reasons an approved student still doesn't receive a payment on time.

Common Problems and How to Fix Them

  • Locked out or forgot your password. Use the Forgot Password option on the myNSFAS login page. You'll usually need your ID number and a one time PIN sent to your registered email or phone. If you're locked out after too many attempts, wait 24 hours before trying again.
  • Stuck on Provisionally Funded. Confirm with your institution's financial aid office that your registration has actually been submitted to NSFAS, this is the most common cause.
  • No payment despite being approved. Check your banking details and NSFAS wallet linkage are correct and active on myNSFAS. If everything looks right and payment still hasn't come through, contact NSFAS directly rather than assuming it will sort itself out.
  • Application stuck for weeks with no update. Make sure every required document was uploaded, since a single missing file can hold up the whole application. If it's genuinely stuck, follow up by phone or email rather than only checking the portal repeatedly.

Watch Out for NSFAS Scams

NSFAS has publicly warned students to only use its official channels, and not to share personal or banking details with accounts that aren't verified. A few things worth keeping in mind.

  • NSFAS will never ask you to pay a fee to check your status, speed up an application, or release an allowance.
  • Only use the official portal at my.nsfas.org.za, the WhatsApp number in this guide, and the toll free line, 0800 067 327. Be cautious of similar looking numbers or websites shared through social media or forwarded messages.
  • Report anything suspicious to NSFAS directly rather than engaging with it, and don't click links in messages you're not sure about.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I check my NSFAS status without a myNSFAS account?

Use WhatsApp or USSD instead. Both only need your ID number, so you can check your status even if you've never logged into the portal or you've forgotten your details.

What does Provisionally Funded actually mean?

It means you've passed NSFAS's own eligibility checks, but your institution still needs to confirm your registration before funding is finalised. It's a good sign, not a rejection, it just isn't the final word yet.

Can I reapply if my NSFAS application was unsuccessful?

Yes, in most cases you can reapply in a future application cycle if you meet the eligibility criteria at that point, or you can appeal your current decision if you believe it was incorrect rather than waiting for the next cycle.

Does NSFAS fund private colleges?

No. NSFAS only funds registered students at public universities and public TVET colleges. If you're studying at a private institution, you won't qualify for NSFAS funding regardless of your household income.

What's the difference between the NSFAS bursary and the NSFAS loan?

The bursary is for households earning R350,000 a year or less, and generally doesn't need to be repaid if you meet academic requirements. The loan option is for the missing middle, households earning above R350,000 up to R600,000, and is repayable once you're working, though it still gives access to funding that wouldn't otherwise be available.

Why is my NSFAS payment late even though I'm approved?

This is most often a banking details or NSFAS wallet issue. Double check these are correct and active on myNSFAS first, then contact NSFAS directly if payment still doesn't arrive.
