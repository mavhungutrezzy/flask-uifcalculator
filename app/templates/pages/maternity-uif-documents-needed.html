Maternity UIF Documents Needed

Prepare the key forms and supporting documents before applying for maternity UIF benefits.

UIF Calculators Team · Updated July 2026 · 5 min read

To claim maternity UIF you will need your ID document, the UI-2.8 banking details form, the UI-2.7 employer form, and either the UI-2.3 application form or a medical certificate, depending on how far along your claim is. Missing paperwork is one of the most common reasons maternity claims are delayed, so it's worth gathering everything up front.

This page lists exactly what the Department of Employment and Labour asks for, plus when you should submit your application.

Maternity UIF Document Checklist

Have these ready before you visit a labour centre or submit online. Some documents depend on whether you're applying before or after the birth.

  • 13-digit bar-coded ID, smart ID card, or valid passport
  • UI-2.8 form for banking details
  • UI-2.7 form for employer remuneration information
  • UI-2.3 maternity application form or medical certificate, depending on the claim stage
  • Medical certificate from a doctor, or the baby's birth certificate
  • UI-4 follow-up form, where required, for continuation of payments

When to Apply

Timeline
Apply before the birth, or within 12 months after the birth of the child.
Eligibility
You must have been employed and contributing to UIF for at least 13 weeks before applying.
Payment condition
Maternity UIF is usually paid only if you receive less than your normal remuneration while on maternity leave.

If your employer continues to pay your full salary during maternity leave, you typically won't qualify for a UIF top-up — the benefit is designed to cover the income gap, not to pay out alongside full pay.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I apply for maternity UIF before the baby is born?

Yes. You can apply before the birth using a medical certificate that confirms your due date, or you can wait and apply afterward using the birth certificate instead.

What happens if I miss the 12-month deadline?

Claims submitted more than 12 months after the birth are generally not accepted, so it's worth applying as early as your documents allow rather than waiting.

Do I need the UI-2.7 form if my employer already submitted it?

If your employer has submitted the UI-2.7 on your behalf, you may not need to resubmit it yourself, but it's worth confirming with the labour centre or your HR department before your appointment.

What is the UI-4 form for?

The UI-4 is a follow-up form used to keep a maternity claim active for continuation of payments, and is only required in specific ongoing-claim situations rather than at initial application.

Will I still get paid if my employer tops up my salary?

It depends on the size of the top-up. UIF maternity benefits are meant to cover the shortfall between your normal salary and what you actually receive while on leave, so a full-salary top-up usually removes the gap UIF would otherwise cover.

Source: Department of Employment and Labour maternity UIF guidance.
