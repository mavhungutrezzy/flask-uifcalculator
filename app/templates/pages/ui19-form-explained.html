UI-19 Form Explained

The UI-19 is your employer's declaration of employment — and one of the most common reasons UIF claims stall. Here's what it covers, who must complete it, and how to avoid the mistakes that delay a payout.

UIF Calculators Team · Updated July 2026 · 9 min read

The UI-19 form is an official document your employer must complete when your employment ends. It declares your employment dates, salary, and reason for termination — the information UIF uses to verify your claim and calculate what you're owed. Without it, UIF has no official record of your employment and cannot process your claim.

What Is the UI-19 Form?

Think of the UI-19 as your employment reference letter for UIF purposes. It contains the essential information UIF needs to verify your work history, salary details, and the circumstances under which you left your job.

Without this form, UIF cannot process your claim because they have no official record of your employment details. The form bridges the gap between your employer's records and the UIF system.

What Is the UI-19 Form Used For?

UIF uses the UI-19 form to verify critical information about your employment and ensure your claim is legitimate and accurate.

  • Employment verification: UIF confirms you were actually employed and for how long, which determines whether you meet the minimum contribution requirements.
  • Salary confirmation: the form confirms your salary details, which directly affects how much UIF you'll receive.
  • Termination details: UIF needs to know why and when you left your job. This determines eligibility and affects processing time.
  • Fraud prevention: the form helps UIF ensure only legitimate employees receive the benefits they're entitled to.

Who Must Complete the UI-19 Form?

The responsibility for completing the UI-19 form falls primarily on the employer, but understanding the process helps you make sure it gets done correctly.

Employer responsibility

Your employer must complete and sign the UI-19 form when your employment ends. This is a legal requirement under South African labour law, and the form should be provided to you free of charge.

Can employees fill it in?

You can help gather the information, but the form must be completed and signed by your employer or an authorized representative. UIF will not accept a UI-19 form completed only by the employee.

What if your employer refuses?

If your employer refuses to complete the form, you can report them to the Department of Labour. UIF may accept alternative documentation in some cases, but this can delay your claim significantly.

When Is the UI-19 Form Required?

The UI-19 form is required in most employment termination scenarios, but what it needs to say changes depending on your situation.

Retrenchment

When you're retrenched, the UI-19 form is essential. Retrenchments qualify for UIF benefits, and the form proves the termination was due to operational requirements. Your employer should provide this form immediately after retrenchment.

Contract ending

When a fixed-term contract reaches its end date, your employer must still issue a UI-19 form. The reason for termination should reflect that the contract expired naturally — not that you resigned or were dismissed — since this affects whether you qualify for benefits. See our guide on UIF for fixed-term contracts for more.

Dismissal

If you were dismissed, your employer must still complete a UI-19 form, whether or not you agree with the dismissal. UIF assesses your claim based on the reason given; disputes over unfair dismissal are handled separately through the CCMA and don't stop you from applying for UIF in the meantime.

Resignation

If you resigned voluntarily, your employer should still give you a UI-19 form, but UIF will generally use it to confirm that you don't qualify for standard unemployment benefits. The exception is constructive dismissal, where resignation was effectively forced — this needs to be established through the CCMA first. Read can I claim UIF if I resigned? for the full picture.

Information Required on the UI-19 Form

The UI-19 form collects comprehensive employment information. Understanding what's required helps you check it for accuracy before it's submitted.

  • Employee details: your full name, ID number, and contact information. These must match your UIF registration details exactly to avoid processing delays.
  • Employment dates: your start and end date of employment. These determine your credit days and how long you can claim benefits.
  • Reason for termination: the specific reason employment ended. This determines eligibility and affects processing time — it should be accurate and honest.
  • Salary information: your monthly salary and any additional earnings, which directly impacts your benefit amount.

Errors on the UI-19 form can reduce your benefits or delay your claim by weeks or months. Double-check all information before submission, and use the UIF calculator to see how your dates and salary affect your payout.

How to Get the UI-19 Form

Getting the UI-19 form should be straightforward, but knowing your options helps if you run into delays or resistance from your employer.

  1. From your employer: the easiest way is to request it directly. They should provide it when you leave the company — ask for it in writing to create a paper trail.
  2. Download from the UIF website: you can download a blank UI-19 form from the official Department of Labour website, print it, and give it to your employer to complete.
  3. uFiling platform: if your employer uses uFiling, they can complete the form online, and you can then access it through your own uFiling account.
  4. UIF office assistance: if your employer delays, visit your nearest UIF office. They can provide the form and may contact your employer on your behalf.

Tip: request the UI-19 form as soon as you know you're leaving your job. This gives you time to follow up if there are any delays.

How to Submit the UI-19 Form to UIF

Submitting the UI-19 form correctly is crucial for timely claim processing. UIF offers a couple of methods depending on what's convenient for you.

Online (uFiling)
The fastest method. Upload a clear scan or photo of the completed form through the uFiling platform — this typically results in faster processing times.
Manual submission
Submit the original form in person at your nearest UIF office, along with your ID and any other required documents. Staff can verify the form immediately.

Submit the UI-19 form when you first register your UIF claim — UIF will not process your claim without it. If you submit it later, your claim processing starts from that later date, not your original application date.

How the UI-19 Form Affects Your UIF Payout

The information on your UI-19 form directly impacts how much UIF you receive and how quickly you get paid. Understanding this connection helps you make sure it's accurate before it's submitted.

  • Salary impact on calculations: the salary listed on your UI-19 form determines your benefit amount. If the salary is incorrect, your benefits will be wrong — use the UIF calculator to check the amount looks right based on your actual salary.
  • Employment dates and credit days: your employment dates determine your credit days. Incorrect dates can reduce your benefit duration or make you ineligible for benefits.
  • Processing delays: errors or missing information can delay your payment by weeks while UIF tries to get clarification from your employer.

Always review the completed UI-19 form carefully before submission. Small errors can have a big impact on how your UIF payout is calculated and when you receive your first payment — see our full guide on how UIF payout is calculated.

What Happens If the UI-19 Form Is Missing or Incorrect?

Missing or incorrect UI-19 forms are among the most common reasons UIF claims get delayed or rejected. Knowing what to expect helps you act quickly if it happens to you.

Claim delays
Without a UI-19 form, UIF cannot process your claim. This can delay your first payment by 4–8 weeks or longer while UIF tries to obtain the form.
Claim rejections
Serious errors or missing signatures can lead to claim rejection. You'll need to correct the issues and reapply, starting the process over.
What you can do
Contact your employer immediately for corrections. If they're unresponsive, visit your nearest UIF office for assistance and possible intervention. If you discover errors after submission, inform UIF in writing — they may require a new, corrected UI-19 form from your employer. Keep copies of all correspondence.

UI-19 Form vs Other UIF Forms

UIF uses several forms for different purposes. Knowing which one applies to you prevents confusion and makes sure you submit the right documentation.

Form Purpose Who completes it When needed
UI-19 Declaration of employment when leaving a job Employer When applying for UIF benefits
UI-2.8 Continuation of UIF claim Employee Monthly, to continue receiving benefits
UI-2.7 Application for maternity benefits Employee and doctor When claiming maternity UIF

The UI-19 form is specifically for unemployment benefits. Other UIF benefits like illness, maternity, or adoption use different forms — see our UIF documents needed guide for the full breakdown by claim type.

Common UI-19 Form Mistakes

Avoiding these common mistakes can save you weeks of claim delays and help make sure you receive the correct benefit amount.

  • Wrong salary: employers sometimes list basic salary only, excluding allowances. This can reduce your UIF benefits — make sure total monthly earnings are correct.
  • Incorrect dates: wrong employment dates affect your credit days and benefit duration. Double-check that start and end dates match your actual employment period.
  • Missing signatures: the form must be signed by an authorized employer representative. Unsigned forms are rejected immediately, causing significant delays.
  • Wrong reason for termination: an incorrect termination reason can affect your eligibility. Make sure it accurately reflects why your employment ended.

Before submitting your UI-19 form, compare the salary and dates against your payslips and employment contract, and use the UIF payout calculator to check the benefit amount seems reasonable.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a UI-19 form?

The UI-19 form is a declaration of employment that employers must complete when employees leave their jobs. It contains employment details, salary information, and termination reasons that UIF uses to process benefit claims.

Who must sign the UI-19 form?

An authorized representative of your employer — usually HR, a payroll administrator, or a director — must sign the UI-19 form. A form signed only by the employee is not valid and will be rejected.

Can I submit a UIF claim without a UI-19 form?

You can open a claim without one, but UIF will not process payment until the form is submitted. If your employer is unresponsive, visit your nearest Labour Centre for help obtaining it.

How long does UIF take to process the UI-19?

Once a correctly completed UI-19 form is submitted, UIF typically processes it within a few weeks as part of the overall claim adjudication. Errors or missing signatures can extend this by 4–8 weeks or more.

Can UIF reject my claim because of the UI-19?

Yes. Missing signatures, incorrect termination reasons, or inconsistent employment dates can lead to rejection. You would need to correct the form with your employer and resubmit it.

Is the UI-19 form required for all UIF claims?

It's required for standard unemployment claims. Maternity, illness, adoption, and dependant's benefits use different forms, like the UI-2.7, though UIF may still request employment confirmation similar to what the UI-19 provides.
