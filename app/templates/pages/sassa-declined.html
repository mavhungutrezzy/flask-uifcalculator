SASSA SRD Declined: Every Reason Explained

Getting declined is stressful, especially when the reason is not clear. This guide explains every SASSA decline reason in plain language and tells you exactly what to do about each one.

SA Benefits & Calculators · Updated August 2026 · 10 min read

Quick answer: Most declines are due to the R624 means test threshold, bank mismatches, or system cross-checks. Check your exact decline status online and appeal within 90 days if incorrect.

All SASSA Decline Reasons

1. Means Test – Income Source Identified

This is the most common reason. SASSA's automated bank screening found that your total deposits for the month were R624 or more. It does not matter what the money was for – a salary, a family transfer, a stokvel payout, a refund – the system adds it all together.

What to do: If the flagged deposits were genuinely not regular income (a one-off family transfer, a refund, etc.), you can appeal the decision. Keep proof of what the deposit actually was.

2. Identity Verification Failed

Your application details do not match what Home Affairs has on record. This could be a spelling difference in your name, an outdated ID number, or a data capture error when you applied.

What to do: Check that the name and ID number on your SRD application match your ID document exactly. If there is a mismatch in Home Affairs records, visit a Home Affairs office to correct it, then appeal with SASSA.

3. Registered on Another System (UIF/NSFAS)

SASSA's cross-check found that you are registered on UIF or NSFAS during the same period. Even if your UIF claim has ended or your NSFAS funding stopped, the systems might not have updated yet.

What to do: If your UIF or NSFAS has genuinely ended, get proof (a closure letter or statement) and submit an appeal with that proof attached.

4. Receiving Another SASSA Grant

You are already receiving another SASSA grant (Older Persons, Disability, Child Support, etc.) and cannot receive SRD at the same time.

What to do: If this is correct, you are not eligible for SRD while receiving another grant. If it is wrong (perhaps the other grant ended), contact SASSA to update your records.

5. Age Criteria Not Met

You are either under 18 or 60 and older. The SRD grant is only for people aged 18 to 59. If you are 60 or older, the Older Persons Grant is the appropriate grant to apply for.

6. Deceased on Home Affairs Records

Home Affairs records show your ID number as deceased. This is an administrative error that unfortunately does happen.

What to do: Visit your nearest Home Affairs office immediately with your ID document to have the error corrected. Once corrected, appeal with SASSA and provide proof from Home Affairs.

7. Not a South African Citizen or Valid Permit Holder

SASSA could not verify your citizenship, permanent residence, refugee status, or special permit. Your documentation may be expired or not on the system.

What to do: Ensure your documentation (asylum seeker permit, refugee status, special permit) is valid and up to date. Visit Home Affairs if needed, then contact SASSA.

Declined Does Not Mean Forever

A critical thing to understand: SASSA assesses each month independently. Being declined in July does not mean you will be declined in August. If the reason for your decline was a one-off event (an unusual deposit that month, for example), the next month might be different.

You do not need to reapply. SASSA automatically reassesses your application each month. Just make sure your details are correct and keep your bank account activity below the R624 threshold.

What to Do Right Now

  1. Check the exact reason. Check your status online – it shows the specific decline reason, not just "declined."
  2. Decide if an appeal makes sense. If the automated system made an error, submit an appeal within 90 days.
  3. Gather proof. Bank statements, letters from Home Affairs, UIF closure letters – anything that supports your case.
  4. Wait for next month. If the decline was caused by a one-off event, next month's assessment may give a different result without you doing anything.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why was I declined when I have no income?

The system checks bank deposits, not just salary. Any money coming into your account – even transfers from family, refunds, or stokvel payouts – counts towards the R624 threshold for that month.

Can I be declined one month and approved the next?

Yes, absolutely. Each month is an independent assessment. If the issue that caused your decline does not repeat, you may be approved the following month.

Do I need to reapply after being declined?

No. Your application stays active. SASSA automatically reassesses you each month. Do not submit a new application – it may cause confusion.

What if I was declined incorrectly?

Submit an appeal within 90 days of the decline. See our appeal guide for the full process.
