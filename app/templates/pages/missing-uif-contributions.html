Missing UIF Contributions or Declarations?

Missing declarations can delay a claim, quietly shrink your visible credit days, or send your file into adjudication while UIF tries to verify your employment history from scratch.

UIF Calculators Team · Updated July 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer: your employer must declare you and pay UIF contributions monthly. If they've fallen behind or missed months, UIF may not have enough of a record to process your claim properly, even though you worked and contributed. It's fixable, but it usually needs your employer to act, not just you.

Every month you work, your employer is meant to declare you to UIF and pay over both your contribution and theirs. Official UIF communications have repeatedly flagged that benefit payments are often delayed when these declarations and contributions aren't up to date. If your claim seems stuck for no reason you can find on your own side, this is one of the first things worth ruling out.

What "Declarations" Actually Means

It's easy to confuse declarations with your UI-19 form, but they're different things. Your UI-19 is a once off document your employer completes when you leave a job. Declarations are ongoing, monthly submissions your employer makes to UIF for every month you're employed, confirming who works for them, what they earn, and paying over the contributions.

This monthly submission is what builds your UIF contribution history and, in turn, your credit days. If a month is missing from that history, it's as if UIF has no record you worked that month at all, regardless of what your payslip says. For the form specific to leaving a job, see our UI-19 form guide.

Signs There May Be a Declaration Problem

  • Your status shows Pending Adjudication. This often means UIF is trying to verify details it can't confirm from existing records.
  • Your credit days look too low for how long you actually worked, given roughly one day of credit builds up for every four days worked.
  • UIF asks for employer documents or salary schedules directly from you, which usually means their own records from your employer are incomplete.
  • Your former employer says they still need to submit information, which is as close to a direct admission as you'll usually get.

What Workers Can Do

  1. Ask your employer directly whether all your monthly declarations were submitted, and for which months, if any, they weren't.
  2. Keep your payslips and any other employment records that show your salary and the dates you worked. These become your evidence if UIF's records and your actual history don't match.
  3. Check your claim status regularly through UIF Online or USSD, since a status that hasn't moved is often the first sign something needs following up.
  4. Contact UIF or visit your nearest Labour Centre if your employer isn't cooperating. UIF can, in some cases, pursue an employer directly for outstanding declarations.

What Happens After You Report It

Once UIF is aware of a declaration gap, your claim typically goes into a verification or adjudication step while they try to reconcile your actual employment history with what's on record. This is why claims with declaration issues often take longer than straightforward ones, UIF isn't just processing paperwork, it's chasing down information that should already have been there.

Providing your own payslips and employment records upfront, rather than waiting to be asked, can speed this along. It gives UIF something concrete to verify against instead of starting from an employer who isn't responding.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it my employer's responsibility to submit UIF declarations, or mine?

It's your employer's legal responsibility. UIF contributions are deducted from your salary and matched by your employer, and it's up to them to declare and pay these over monthly.

Can I check whether my employer is up to date with declarations?

There isn't always a direct self service check for this specifically, but a low or unexpected credit day count when you check your status is usually the clearest sign. Asking your employer directly, in writing, is the most reliable way to get a straight answer.

What if my employer refuses to fix missing declarations?

Visit your nearest Labour Centre with your payslips and employment records. UIF has the ability to follow up with employers directly on outstanding declarations, and providing your own evidence helps them act on it.

Will missing declarations permanently reduce my credit days?

Not necessarily. Once the missing months are declared and reconciled, your credit days should update to reflect your actual contribution history. The issue is usually timing and follow up, not a permanent loss, provided the employer eventually submits what's owed.

Does a missing declaration affect my UI-19 form too?

Not directly, they're separate documents. But if your employer has been inconsistent with monthly declarations, it's worth double checking your UI-19 carefully as well, since the same administrative gaps that cause one often show up in the other.
